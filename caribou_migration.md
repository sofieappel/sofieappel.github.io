# Caribou Migration in Alaska


Caribou have the longest migration of any land mammal on the planet, often covering over 2,000 miles every year (Trustees). Their migration is mainly motivated by their reproduction patterns and the need to avoid predators during their calving seasons, typically in late May and early June (NPS). 

This project investigates the migratory patterns of a subspecies of caribou, the *Rangifer tarandus granti*. This subspecies is one of the largest in North America. 

Because caribou are herd animals, it is challenging to effectively plot their migration with the data that is available. The data for caribou on gbif.org is all human observation, meaning there are many more observations during summer months. Therefore, data over a range of years (2004-2025) were used to produce investigate caribou migration. The data were grouped by month with the knowledge that caribou migration is a consistent yearly pattern. Further investigation into how caribou migration has changed over time due to changes in climate would be interesting, but difficult because of the small amount of data available.
